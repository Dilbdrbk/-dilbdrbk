04 Method

Map the entity first, then build the pages

See a full example

Every site I plan starts as a map: the central entity, its attributes, and a decision for each one about whether it gets its own page.

  1. 01

    Define the entity

    Name the central entity and the source context: what the business is and how it earns.

  2. 02

    List its attributes

    Types, problems, costs, locations and brands: everything people search about it.

  3. 03

    Page or section

    An attribute gets its own page only with its own demand, intent and unique substance.

  4. 04

    Link it together

    Core pages earn, outer pages support them, and internal links connect both.

05 Research & method

Research and method behind the work

The ideas behind every strategy I write.

See them as diagrams

  • 01

    Search reads entities, not just words

    I structure a site around its central entity and the attributes people search for, so its meaning is easy to understand.

  • 02

    The query is processed first

    Search engines rewrite and expand a query before ranking anything. A page has to match the intent behind the words.

  • 03

    Every page has to earn its place

    A topic gets its own page only when it has its own intent and enough substance. Otherwise it becomes a section.

  • 04

    One fact per sentence

    Clear subject, predicate and object make each sentence easy for machines to parse, connect and quote.

  • 05

    Answer first

    The first lines of a page or section answer its question. Background and detail come after.

  • 06

    Built for snippets and AI answers

    Plain facts, clear lists and structured data make a passage easy to lift into a snippet or an AI answer.

  • 07

    Lower the cost of retrieval

    Clean architecture, fewer thin pages and direct answers make a site cheaper to crawl and understand.

  • 08

    Go to the source

    Google's patents and whitepapers explain the signals behind rankings better than most summaries of them.

  • 09

    Test before you trust

    An SEO idea is a hypothesis until a controlled test shows it moves rankings.

06 Habits

How I work and keep learning

What I do every week to keep my SEO current and my calls grounded in evidence.

  1. 01

    Follow the right experts for each vertical

    Before I work in a new vertical, I decide which SEO experts and sources to follow for it.

  2. 02

    Separate myths from facts with A/B tests

    I run SEO A/B tests so decisions rest on results, not on popular claims.

  3. 03

    Read official statements critically

    Search engine statements are a source, not the final word. I check them against tests and data before relying on them.

  4. 04

    Watch algorithm updates every day

    I check volatility sensors such as Semrush Sensor and AccuRanker's Grump daily to spot updates early.

  5. 05

    Analyze the SERP, then validate

    I study the results page and the site, form an inference, and test it before calling it a finding.

  6. 06

    Think like a search engine

    I ask how a search engine would crawl, understand and rank a page, then plan the work around that.

  7. 07

    Read patents, papers and official documentation

    Search engine patents, research papers and official statements show where search is heading.

  8. 08

    Keep learning code

    I study front-end and back-end development, data science, machine learning and natural language processing.

  9. 09

    Communicate clearly with clients

    Clear, regular updates keep clients informed and projects on track, so I keep improving how I explain the work.

  10. 10

    Read the SEO newsletters that matter

    Newsletters such as SEOFOMO keep me current between projects.
